## Chapter 33: The Allelic Profile – Elongated Skulls and Human Variation
In 1805, when **Meriwether Lewis** and **William Clark** reached the mouth of the **Columbia River** after two years of walking across a continent, they found the **Chinook** waiting for them. The Chinook were shrewd traders, skilled navigators, and the economic middlemen of the Pacific Northwest. They were also, by the standards of every European who encountered them, physically striking in a way that no Virginia education had prepared the Corps of Discovery to understand. The free-born Chinook had skulls that rose from the brow at an angle that would have alarmed any modern pediatrician, the result of a procedure begun in the first weeks of life: a board strapped to the infant's forehead and secured by bindings, left in place month after month while the cranial bones were still soft enough to yield. The practice was not cosmetic. It was political. A round skull, in the Pacific Northwest of 1805, was the mark of a slave.

**Chief Comcomly** of the Chinook was the most famous practitioner of this tradition, a one-eyed diplomat of considerable intelligence who so impressed the Lewis and Clark expedition that he became, for a time, one of the two most famous Native Americans in the young republic, alongside **Sacagawea**. **Washington Irving**, America's first international bestselling author, described the process of head-binding in his 1836 book ***Astoria***, which remained required reading in American schools for over sixty years: "The appearance of the infant, however, while in this state of compression, is whimsically hideous, and its little black eyes, we are told, being forced out by the tightness of the bandages, resemble those of a mouse choked in a trap." Irving devoted extensive passages to Comcomly himself, painting a portrait of a man who was at once a shrewd trader, an intelligent strategist, a careful negotiator, and a brilliant orator. Comcomly repeatedly talked younger warriors out of attacking the American newcomers: Do you see how nice their clothes are? Do you see how cool their weapons are? Do you see any of their women or children? What do you think is going to happen when they bring in their families? He was not a pacifist. He was a strategist who understood that his people had not yet seen what these newcomers were capable of. He married three of his daughters to influential fur traders, including the commander of **Fort Astoria**, **Duncan McDougall**, in political marriages that consolidated Chinook influence over the regional trade network. Nobody accused him of progressive politics.

Comcomly was so central to relations between the Chinook and the arriving Americans that when Fort Astoria was relocated thirty miles upriver from his village, he openly wept.

Comcomly's elongated skull was not incidental to his authority. It was, in the cultural grammar of his people, the physical proof that he was born free, that he was worthy of leadership, that the spirits had marked him for something higher than servitude. And he believed that his elongated skull was what made him intelligent.

After Comcomly died, his skull was studied for nearly a century before being repatriated. The Smithsonian Institution published a monograph on it in 1960, titled ***The Chinook Sign of Freedom***. Today, the Smithsonian's own archives lack a photograph of the cover.

This is the first problem with elongated skulls. Not the skulls themselves, which are real, documented, and found on every inhabited continent. The problem is that the closer you look at the evidence, the harder it becomes to see.

Richards lives in the Pacific Northwest, less than three hundred miles from where Comcomly once held court. He had never heard of the man until he started researching **cranial modification**. This seemed strange. It seemed stranger when he discovered that the practice was not a minor curiosity confined to a single tribe but a defining cultural feature of virtually every free community west of the **Cascade Mountains**.<sup>[1](../ENDNOTES.md#sources-chapter-33)</sup><sup>[2](../ENDNOTES.md#sources-chapter-33)</sup><sup>[3](../ENDNOTES.md#sources-chapter-33)</sup><sup>[4](../ENDNOTES.md#sources-chapter-33)</sup>

### The Vanishing

Lewis and Clark recorded hundreds of modified skulls on their expedition. European visitors in the 1790s documented burial sites containing roughly three thousand elongated-skull remains at a single location near present-day **Longview, Washington**. The Chinook, the **Salish**, the **Cowlitz**, the **Chehalis**: all practiced cranial modification. Tribes east of the Cascades referred to their western neighbors collectively as "flatheads." The practice was so deeply embedded that when Comcomly himself suggested abandoning it, his people refused to discuss the matter. Everyone would get up and leave the room, he reported, until he was talking to a few children and old women. This was the one subject his people would not permit him to raise.

And then, over the course of about a century, this entire cultural tradition was edited out of the public record.

Richards found that searching "elongated skulls" online funneled him invariably to **Paracas, Peru**. The Pacific Northwest skulls, despite being more numerous and better documented in contemporaneous written accounts, were effectively invisible. Images of Chinook people in museum displays and roadside markers depicted them with perfectly ordinary craniums, indistinguishable from the stock Hollywood Indian of a 1960s television western. Richards' colleague **Sammy**, a videographer for **WildWolf Productions**, had studied Native American culture at the **University of Oregon** and completed his entire degree without once hearing the practice mentioned. It was happening there, on the land where the university stands, two centuries earlier, and no one in the anthropology department saw fit to bring it up.

Even the search tools seemed to resist the inquiry. When Richards entered the exact latitude and longitude of a **memaluse**, the Chinook word for burial ground, along with a reference to the Wikipedia page documenting the site's existence, Google still redirected him to the one easy-to-find location: the state park, the thing everybody already knew about. A lot like Paracas, he was being funneled to the popular site.

The burial sites fared worse. **Mount Coffin**, a rock formation along the Columbia River near Longview that once held an estimated three thousand canoe burials, each containing an elongated skull, was demolished in 1841 by **Lieutenant Charles Wilkes** during the **United States Exploring Expedition**. Wilkes, a man of such temperament that some scholars believe he inspired **Captain Ahab** in *Moby Dick*, was convicted of excessive punishment against his own men and led expeditions with a results-at-any-cost efficiency. He ordered the site cleared with no systematic recording of its contents. European visitors who had seen Mount Coffin before Wilkes arrived described a bedrock partially covered by forest and entirely covered by grave-boats, watched over by Cowlitz warriors. After Wilkes, all of it was gone.

The skulls that survived entered private collections or vanished. By the 1830s, the **phrenology** craze had created a market for unusual skulls, and the Pacific Northwest became a hunting ground. The city of Longview was later built around the Mount Coffin site by **R.A. Long**, a timber baron and prominent **Freemason** who financed an entire planned city to house workers for his mill. Whatever remained beneath the construction was buried under infrastructure.

Today, there are no elongated Chinook skulls available for public viewing.

### The Peruvian Parallel

Five thousand miles to the south, in the caves near Paracas, Peru, a parallel story was unfolding with a cast of characters that reads less like anthropology and more like a screenplay that keeps getting rewritten by people who cannot agree on the genre.

The **Paracas Necropolis** was first excavated in the 1920s by **Julio C. Tello**, often called the father of Peruvian archaeology. He found hundreds of **funerary bundles**: multi-layered textile bags containing a deceased person wrapped in grave goods. Many of the skulls inside were elongated, some dramatically so. The largest documented specimen has a **cranial capacity** of 1,655 cubic centimeters, more than ten percent above the modern male average of 1,380 cc, and measures twenty-eight centimeters in length. Some lacked the **sagittal suture**, the seam that normally runs along the top of the human skull where the two **parietal bones** fuse during infancy. Some had red hair. Some showed evidence of trepanation, holes drilled into the living skull, a procedure that many patients survived, judging by bone regrowth around the margins.

These are facts. What happened to these facts after their discovery is where the record becomes difficult to follow.

Tello's initial count of the funerary bundles was 429. When he first spoke publicly about the find, the number was 451. In a 1933 paper on government underfunding, 451 again. A co-authored paper confirmed 451. The discrepancy was attributed to nearby cave burials assigned numbers in the middle of the main sequence. The explanation raises its own questions without resolving the arithmetic.

The bundles were transported from the excavation site to a museum in **Lima**. Some were opened along the way because they were "deteriorating." Records of those roadside openings are thin to nonexistent. When Tello needed funding, he secured a meeting with **Nelson Rockefeller**, who visited Peru in 1937. Rockefeller donated three thousand dollars, and in exchange, four funerary bundles were shipped to the Metropolitan Museum of Art in New York, where Rockefeller sat on the board. Tello stated that the four bundles selected were the ones in worst condition, the ones most urgently needing conservation. The three thousand dollars was then spent preserving 346 textiles. Not bundles. Textiles. The bundles that had just been described as falling apart were apparently not in sufficient disrepair to warrant spending any of the Rockefeller money on their actual preservation.

The Rockefeller donation had an unintended political consequence. **General Benavides**, the military dictator then running Peru, reportedly took note: "What is good enough for Rockefeller is good enough for Peru," and offered Tello financing for a new museum. The story goes that Benavides even tried to buy some of the recently preserved textiles, and Tello told him no. Richards, who dug through this history in detail, was blunt about this claim: Benavides was a military dictator who was not above disappearing people. The idea that an archaeologist who had just received funding for his pet project would refuse a direct request from a man who could have him shot on the museum floor does not pass a basic credibility test.

In 1949, one bundle was shipped to the United States for a public opening. It was packed for air transport, did not fit on the plane, was sent by ship instead, and arrived twenty-two days later. **Junius Bird**, the anthropologist present at its arrival, noted that it lacked a musty smell. A sealed bag containing a human body, packed in fabric, at sea for three weeks, with no odor. This is either a miracle of ancient embalming or evidence that the contents were not what they were supposed to be.

In 1967, Tello's successor **Toribio Mejia Xesspe** opened fifty-four bundles and recorded the contents of six. The other forty-eight: no record of which bundles were opened, what they contained, or what happened to anything found inside. At least eighteen bundles were stolen outright from the site in 1951. By the 1950s, Tello's successor **Rebecca Carrion Cachot** estimated that over seventy percent of Peruvian artifacts were housed outside of Peru. Berlin alone held nearly fifty thousand items.

The pattern is consistent. At every stage, from discovery to preservation to study, the most interesting specimens either disappeared, were inadequately documented, or were studied by people who recorded nothing. It is not necessary to invoke a conspiracy to explain this. Financial incentive, institutional neglect, and the colonial-era attitude that indigenous remains were curiosities to be traded rather than evidence to be preserved can account for most of it. But the cumulative effect is the same regardless of intent: the data that would answer the most important questions about these skulls is gone, and it was gone before anyone with modern analytical tools could examine it.

As Richards summarized it: the most mysterious specimens that came from that site will probably never be in public hands again. And that is sad, because there is something genuinely enigmatic about them. The data from the alternative researchers is just as threadbare. We are not getting data. We are getting the assertion that there is data.

### The Three Hypotheses

The elongated skulls found worldwide are explained by three hypotheses. Each is partially correct. None is complete.

**The mainstream position** is that all elongated skulls are the product of **artificial cranial deformation** (or **ACD**, as it is known in academic circles), a cultural practice documented across at least forty societies over ten thousand years. The mechanism is well understood: binding applied to infant skulls during the first months of life, while the cranial bones are still pliable, reshapes the vault without increasing total volume. The **NIH** has published peer-reviewed studies on the practice as observed in **Tiwanaku, Bolivia**, demonstrating that specific binding techniques produce specific, reproducible skull shapes. Suture patterns, binding marks, and volumetric measurements allow trained physical anthropologists to distinguish artificial deformation from natural pathological elongation caused by conditions like **craniosynostosis** or familial **macrocephaly**.

Sklyarov ([Chapter 1](01-the-tolerance-problem.md)), despite his own very different conclusions, actually confirmed this mechanism clearly. In *Перу и Боливия задолго до инков* he documented the physical evidence: binding cloths found in South American burials, wooden boards (дощечки) found in Mesoamerican graves, and even specialized infant cradles designed to prevent children from removing the apparatus. He traced the practice across the **Chavin**, **Lauricocha**, Paracas, **Nazca**, **Puerto Moorin**, and Inca cultures. The archaeological evidence for intentional deformation, at least for the majority of specimens, is not in dispute. Attempting to dispute it is a waste of time.<sup>[5](../ENDNOTES.md#sources-chapter-33)</sup><sup>[6](../ENDNOTES.md#sources-chapter-33)</sup><sup>[7](../ENDNOTES.md#sources-chapter-33)</sup><sup>[8](../ENDNOTES.md#sources-chapter-33)</sup><sup>[9](../ENDNOTES.md#sources-chapter-33)</sup><sup>[10](../ENDNOTES.md#sources-chapter-33)</sup><sup>[11](../ENDNOTES.md#sources-chapter-33)</sup><sup>[12](../ENDNOTES.md#sources-chapter-33)</sup><sup>[13](../ENDNOTES.md#sources-chapter-33)</sup><sup>[14](../ENDNOTES.md#sources-chapter-33)</sup>

**The alternative position**, championed by Brien Foerster, a tour-company operator with no relevant scientific credentials, holds that some Paracas skulls represent a non-human species or subspecies, based on DNA analysis that purportedly revealed anomalous genetic signatures. Foerster announced these results on social media. The geneticist he cited refused to be publicly identified. No peer-reviewed paper supports the claim. The one **STR analysis** published in a peer-reviewed journal (the *Journal of Biotechnology & Bioinformatics Research*) found **allelic profiles** fully consistent with modern human populations and no foreign or anomalous DNA. This is the **allelic profile** that gives this chapter its name: the published, verifiable genetic data points unambiguously to *Homo sapiens*.

Richards, who investigated both sides of the Paracas story with equal skepticism, was characteristically direct about Foerster's claims: that "we had them tested and this is what it said" approach, with no links, no published data, no verifiable chain of custody, is the same epistemic standard as claiming your girlfriend lives in Canada and that is why nobody can meet her. It is never going to fly.<sup>[15](../ENDNOTES.md#sources-chapter-33)</sup><sup>[16](../ENDNOTES.md#sources-chapter-33)</sup>

But Richards was equally harsh on the 2022 peer-reviewed paper that attempted to debunk the anomalous-DNA claims. The researchers tested a single skull, and it was visibly one of the least anomalous specimens in the collection: a skull that even Foerster would have classified as ordinary artificial deformation. Testing the most boring skull in the room and declaring the mystery solved is not rigorous science either. As Richards put it: since when is testing one skull any sort of evidence? There are over four hundred bundles. You cannot find five or ten to test? Replicability, man. Give me multiple test samples.

### The Sklyarov Taxonomy

**The third position** is the one that nobody holds officially but that the evidence keeps circling back to. It was articulated most completely by Sklyarov, across multiple books: *Обитаемый остров Земля*, ***Какова ты, родина богов***, ***Наска: гигантские рисунки на полях***, and *Перу и Боливия задолго до инков*. The argument runs roughly like this.<sup>[17](../ENDNOTES.md#sources-chapter-33)</sup>

Among the thousands of elongated skulls in the record, three distinct categories can be identified by physical characteristics. Sklyarov laid out the taxonomy explicitly: «Вытянутые черепа могут относиться сразу к трем вариантам: черепа самих "яйцеголовых богов"; черепа их потомков-полукровок; черепа людей, "замаскированных" под богов при помощи искусственной деформации.» (Elongated skulls may belong to three categories at once: skulls of the "egg-headed gods" themselves; skulls of their half-breed descendants; and skulls of humans "disguised" as gods through artificial deformation.)<sup>[18](../ENDNOTES.md#sources-chapter-33)</sup><sup>[19](../ENDNOTES.md#sources-chapter-33)</sup>

Strip away the extraterrestrial framework and what remains is an observational taxonomy that the mainstream has not proposed an alternative to.

First, skulls with normal cranial volume but elongated shape, bearing clear marks of external compression: artificial deformations, the mainstream explanation, no argument.

Second, skulls with increased cranial volume, elongated shape, and no visible compression marks. Sklyarov was precise about the physical argument: «Вышеупомянутой изуверской процедурой с досками можно добиться удлиненной формы головы, но нельзя увеличить объем черепной коробки!» (By the aforementioned barbaric procedure with boards one can achieve an elongated head shape, but one cannot increase the volume of the cranial vault!) He pointed to the work of **Robert Connolly**, who in 1995 published measurements and photographs of South American skulls with volumes he claimed were nearly double the human average. Sklyarov acknowledged that some of Connolly's specimens showed possible traces of binding and that the claims warranted independent verification: «строго говоря, выводы Конноли об аномальности черепа за номером 176 стоило бы перепроверить» (strictly speaking, Connolly's conclusions about the anomaly of skull number 176 ought to be re-checked). The verification has not happened.

Third, skulls with absent or anomalous suture patterns, particularly the missing sagittal suture observed in specimens at the **Museum of Archaeology** in **Valletta, Malta** and in some Paracas examples. The sagittal suture is a structural feature of human cranial development tied to the mechanics of birth-canal passage. Its absence presents a genuine anatomical puzzle that binding does not explain.

Sklyarov noted that distinguishing between the three categories is, in principle, straightforward: «по имеющимся характерным признакам, в виде отличия объема черепной коробки, формы, следов внешнего воздействия и тому подобному, вполне возможно выделить из общей массы находок черепа каждой группы.» (Based on existing characteristic features, such as differences in cranial vault volume, shape, traces of external impact and so forth, it is entirely possible to separate skulls of each group from the general mass of finds.) And then the sentence that quietly indicts the entire field: «Но такие исследования пока никто не проводил.» (But no one has yet conducted such research.)

That sentence was written over a decade ago. It remains true.

### The Dreadful Choice

Sklyarov noticed something else in the data. In Mesoamerican and South American populations that practiced both trepanation and cranial deformation, the two procedures showed an inverse social distribution. He laid this out in ***Наска***: «Низшим сословиям доставалась трепанация, а представителям знати (или аристократии) – деформация черепа. И вот, что примечательно: среди деформированных черепов практически нет трепанированных. Они попадаются буквально совсем в штучных количествах и представляют из себя скорее исключение, нежели правило.» (The lower classes got trepanation, while the nobility, or aristocracy, got cranial deformation. And here is what is notable: among deformed skulls there are practically no trepanned ones. They appear in literally single-digit quantities and are more the exception than the rule.)

This is a strange pattern if you assume both practices are purely medical or purely cosmetic. If trepanation were therapeutic, you would expect it to be performed on whoever was sick, regardless of social class, and you would especially expect it among elites, who had better access to medical practitioners. If cranial deformation were aesthetic, you would expect it to be distributed more evenly, as aesthetic practices tend to be, rather than rigidly stratified by social rank. Sklyarov made this point explicitly: if trepanation were for medical purposes, you would expect no social skew, and if anything, more complex operations would be performed on the upper classes, not fewer.

He also pressed a question that mainstream archaeology has largely sidestepped: why did so many cultures, separated by thousands of kilometers and millennia of history, converge on exactly the same target shape? In *Перу и Боливия задолго до инков* he wrote: «Почему при самых разнообразных способах воздействия на форму головы и на самых разных территориях, где была распространена подобная практика, люди старались обеспечить один и тот же результат – добиться именно удлиненной формы головы? Никто, нигде и никогда не стремился к какой-то иной форме – скажем, сплюснутой, треугольной или квадратной.» (Why, with the most diverse methods of influencing head shape, and across the most diverse territories where the practice was widespread, did people strive for one and the same result: precisely an elongated head shape? No one, nowhere, ever strove for some other shape, say, flattened, triangular, or square.)

The mainstream response is convergent cultural evolution: independent populations arrived at the same practice by coincidence. This is possible. It is also the least testable explanation available, which is what makes it so durable in academic arguments. You cannot falsify coincidence.

Sklyarov's own explanation involved «яйцеголовые боги» (egg-headed gods) who conducted neurological experiments on humans via trepanation, and whose human subjects attempted to protect their children by reshaping their heads to resemble the gods: «Люди стремились "замаскировать" своих детей под детей "богов", меняя им форму головы.» (People sought to "disguise" their children as children of the "gods" by changing the shape of their heads.) The analogy he drew had a certain dark force: «Чем нейрохирургические эксперименты богов над людьми отличаются от тех экспериментов, которые проводят сами люди в лабораториях над мышами, собаками и даже обезьянами?» (How do the neurosurgical experiments of the gods on humans differ from those experiments that humans themselves conduct in laboratories on mice, dogs, and even monkeys?)

This is, again, a speculative leap that outruns the evidence by a considerable margin. But the underlying observation, that deformation and trepanation are inversely correlated along social-class lines in the same populations, is a datum that the mainstream explanation has not addressed. And his observation about the uniformity of target shape across cultures is a genuine puzzle that "convergent evolution" does not so much solve as name.

### The Egyptian Thread

Sklyarov extended the pattern to **Egypt**, and here his work overlaps with observations from the Cosmic Summit Channel and multiple other researchers. The daughters of the pharaoh Akhenaten are consistently depicted with dramatically elongated skulls, and they lived to healthy adulthood, which rules out pathological hydrocephaly. Akhenaten himself is always shown in a tall headpiece that could, Sklyarov argued, have been concealing the same cranial shape. He noted that Egyptian gods who were said to have ruled Egypt directly, after the Flood but thousands of years before the pharaohs, are depicted wearing headgear shaped to accommodate elongated skulls. The older god **Ptah**, whose reign was said to predate the Flood, is depicted with an ordinary head, which, Sklyarov drily observed, «не удивительно, поскольку он был "богом, удалившимся на небеса", и его никто из египтян не видел» (is not surprising, since he was a "god who departed for the heavens" and no Egyptian had ever seen him). The gods people had supposedly seen in person got the egg-shaped headgear. The gods from before living memory got an ordinary skull. Whatever you make of the underlying theory, the iconographic pattern is there.

At the Hypogeum of Hal-Saflieni in Malta, one of the oldest underground structures in the world, excavators discovered dozens of elongated skulls alongside thousands of other remains. One of these skulls is now on display at the **Museum of Archaeology in Valletta**. As documented by the Cosmic Summit Channel, this particular skull lacks the sagittal suture entirely and is elongated far beyond standard skull size. The Hypogeum itself is a restricted site: visitors surrender their phones and electronics, are permitted to see roughly twenty percent of the complex, and the remaining seventy percent is sealed from public access.

The skull in Valletta, the daughters of Akhenaten in the **Berlin Museum** and Cairo Museum, the woman from the «склеп царицы» (crypt of the queen) in Palenque, Mexico, the burial sites of the Pacific Northwest, the caves of Paracas, the tombs at 14,000 feet in the **Peruvian highlands** where **Pillars of the Past** documented elongated skulls still sitting in open burial chambers: the geographic range is too wide, the chronological span too deep, and the morphological consistency too uniform for any single explanation to feel complete.

### The Missing Map

The geographic distribution of cranial modification is, by itself, one of the more interesting unsolved puzzles in physical anthropology. The practice appears in the Pacific Northwest, in Peru, in Mesoamerica, in Egypt, in Malta, in Tonga, in Hawaii, in China, and in parts of Europe. Richards also turned up something that most researchers had missed entirely. The distribution of cranial modification across the **Pacific Islands** had been quietly dropped from the academic record. A 1938 survey by **Eric John Dingwall** had mapped the practice comprehensively across the globe, including extensive documentation of Pacific Island sites. A 2019 paper on the same topic, titled "Earliest known intentionally deformed human cranium from Asia," reproduced a world distribution map with the Pacific Islands almost entirely omitted. The omission means that a generation of scholars working from that paper will not know to look for connections between Pacific cranial modification and the better-known South American and Egyptian examples.<sup>[20](../ENDNOTES.md#sources-chapter-33)</sup>

This is not a minor cartographic oversight. Hawaii alone, according to a 1980 study examining seventy-six pre-contact indigenous skulls, showed cranial modification in nearly nine percent of specimens. **Tonga** produced what may be the most extreme specimen in the record: a skull ninety-six percent as wide as it is long. That skull was looted in the 1830s, repatriated in 2024, and remains unavailable for public viewing. The museum that received it will publish measurements but will not release photographs or even drawings. The stated reason is a commitment to "decolonize museums and quit treating human remains as curiosities." Academics can still access the images. The public that pays the taxes to keep these institutions open cannot.<sup>[21](../ENDNOTES.md#sources-chapter-33)</sup>

The **kelp highway hypothesis** offers a mechanism for those connections. The hypothesis, now supported by a January 2026 paper on the distribution of **kelp forests** along the South American coast over the past twenty thousand years, proposes that the initial human settlement of the western Americas occurred by boat, with seafaring populations hopping along the coastline and following kelp-supported marine ecosystems. The coast of Peru and Chile had stable kelp forests for eons before the Younger Dryas, making these areas oases of ecological stability. If you are a hunter-gatherer community, that stability is a very big deal.

Richards connected the dots that had been lying in plain sight. The Pacific Northwest and Peru both practiced cranial modification. Both maintained elaborate above-ground or cave-based burial systems where descendants could see the remains of their ancestors, reinforcing the tradition across generations. The Chinook buried their dead in canoes, either elevated on scaffolds or in driftwood-and-log catacombs above ground. The Paracas wrapped their dead in textile bundles and placed them in open caves. In both cases, when you buried your father, you saw your grandfather. The visual continuity of elongated skulls across generations was a physical, tangible anchor for the practice.<sup>[22](../ENDNOTES.md#sources-chapter-33)</sup>

Both regions also sit on the kelp highway corridor. Both have DNA evidence (in the case of Easter Island, confirmed by a recent study showing both Polynesian and South American genetic signatures in pre-contact populations) suggesting sustained maritime contact across vast distances. The Candelabra of the Andes, a geoglyph six hundred feet tall and two hundred feet wide, is carved into the desert hillside five miles from the Paracas caves where the elongated skulls were found. It faces the sea. It is visible from twelve miles offshore. Whether it was a navigational marker, a territorial claim, or something else entirely, it was made to be seen by people arriving by water.

Richards also pointed to the moai of Easter Island. The islanders did not practice cranial modification on the living, at least not according to any surviving record. But the moai themselves have elongated, pointed heads. And they marked the skulls of their deceased with patterns that sometimes resemble those found in South American contexts. Easter Island sits at what could be the midpoint of a trans-Pacific route, a logical place to locate what many ancient cultures called an axis mundi or omphalos: a world-navel, the center of creation, the point where divine power first touched the earth.

The idea that the Pacific was a navigated ocean rather than an impassable barrier is not fringe science. The settlement of Australia, accepted at roughly 65,000 years ago, required sustained ocean voyaging. If people could cross open water to reach Australia, there is nothing physically implausible about kelp-highway navigation along the western coast of the Americas. The Clovis-first hypothesis, which for decades insisted that the Americas were settled exclusively via the Bering Land Bridge around 12,000 years ago, was dismantled by evidence from **Monte Verde, Chile** and other sites. The archaeologist who proved it, Tom Dillehay, had a colleague threaten his family through the local newspaper during the Pinochet dictatorship over the political implications of the finding. The resistance to pre-Clovis settlement was not scientific skepticism. It was institutional enforcement of a preferred narrative, complete with what Richards and others call the Clovis Mafia or the Clovis Police: archaeologists and anthropologists who targeted their own colleagues for presenting inconvenient evidence.

The pattern should sound familiar by now.

### The Open Ledger

The honest summary of the elongated-skull question is shorter than any participant in the debate would prefer.

Artificial cranial deformation is a real, well-documented, widespread cultural practice. This is not in dispute. The majority of elongated skulls in the archaeological record are products of this practice.

A subset of elongated skulls display features, specifically increased cranial volume and anomalous suture morphology, that are not easily explained by binding alone. This subset has never been studied systematically, in part because the specimens have been lost, sold, restricted from access, or inadequately catalogued over the past century and a half.

The DNA evidence that would resolve the question of whether any elongated skulls represent a non-*Homo sapiens* population does not exist in any verifiable, peer-reviewed form. The claims that such evidence exists are unsubstantiated. The one peer-reviewed genetic study found nothing anomalous, and it tested the least interesting skull available.

The cultural practice of cranial modification is globally distributed in a pattern that correlates with coastal and maritime populations. The mechanism for cultural transmission along these routes, the kelp highway, is increasingly well-supported. The belief that cranial modification conferred cognitive or spiritual benefits was held independently by populations on multiple continents. Whether "independently" means "without contact" or "along a connected maritime corridor" is exactly the question that the omission of Pacific Island data from modern surveys prevents researchers from investigating.

The inverse correlation between trepanation and cranial deformation across social strata, documented by Sklyarov in Mesoamerican and South American contexts, is a pattern that exists in the data and has not been addressed by the mainstream. The uniformity of target shape across cultures, always elongation, never any other geometry, is a puzzle that "convergent evolution" names but does not explain.

The institutional handling of elongated skull specimens, from the Smithsonian to the Peruvian national museum to the Australian repatriation programs, has been characterized by lost records, undocumented openings, restricted access, and a consistent pattern of the most anomalous specimens disappearing into private collections. Whether this pattern reflects deliberate suppression or ordinary institutional dysfunction is, at this point, underdetermined. Both explanations are compatible with the evidence. Neither is fully satisfying. The recent discovery of Homo floresiensis, a small-bodied hominin that lived on an island in Indonesia, confirmed that genuinely different human species coexisted with *Homo sapiens* far more recently than previously believed. As the Cosmic Summit researchers noted, that was the most amazing mainstream finding of the last twenty-five years: there was a little different kind of human that lived on an island.

The skulls exist. The practice existed. The questions remain open. And the evidence that would close them keeps getting harder to find.
