# Part VI: The Reckoning


## Chapter 34: The Overkill Debate – Ancient Genetics, Human Origins, and Megafauna Coexistence
In the summer of 2010, a Swedish geneticist named **Svante Paabo** sat in his office at the **Max Planck Institute for Evolutionary Anthropology** in Leipzig, Germany, staring at a sequence of nucleotides that should not have existed. For three decades, Paabo had been trying to extract usable DNA from ancient bones, a project that most of his colleagues considered, at best, quixotic. Ancient DNA degrades. It fragments. It gets contaminated by the bacteria that consume the corpse, by the archaeologists who handle the bones, by the lab technicians who prepare the samples. Every year since the 1980s, someone had announced they had extracted DNA from some impossibly old specimen, a magnolia leaf, an amber-trapped insect, a dinosaur bone, and every year the result turned out to be contamination. Paabo knew this better than anyone. He had published papers debunking several of these claims himself. He had developed an entire methodology around the problem, a set of clean-room protocols and statistical tests designed to distinguish genuine ancient sequences from modern contamination, and had watched other researchers fail to meet those standards over and over.

But the bone fragment sitting in his lab was different. It had come from **Denisova Cave**, a limestone cavity in the Altai Mountains of southern Siberia, where a Russian archaeologist named **Anatoly Derevianko** had found it in 2008 among a scattering of stone tools and animal remains. The cave had been excavated intermittently since the 1970s and had already yielded Neanderthal artifacts, making it one of the key sites for understanding the distribution of archaic humans across northern Asia. The fragment was tiny, a piece of a distal phalanx from the fifth finger of what appeared to be a child, perhaps five to seven years old. Russian researchers had initially assumed it was from a modern human or possibly a Neanderthal, both of which had been found in the region. They sent it to Leipzig almost as an afterthought, one more bone among dozens shipped for routine analysis.

When Paabo's team sequenced the mitochondrial DNA, the result was so unexpected that they ran it again. And then again. The sequence diverged from modern humans at nearly twice the rate of Neanderthal DNA, suggesting a lineage that had split from the human family tree roughly a million years ago. This was not a human. This was not a Neanderthal. This was something else entirely, a hominin species that had never been identified from fossils, that had no name, that had left no tools distinctive enough to mark its presence archaeologically, and that would have remained invisible to science forever if not for the molecular evidence locked inside a single fragment of bone smaller than a thumbnail. Paabo and his team called them the **Denisovans**, after the cave where the bone was found.

The paper appeared in *Nature* in December 2010. Within four years, the implications had rewritten the story of human origins more thoroughly than any archaeological discovery in half a century.


### The Ghost in the Genome

The Denisovan discovery was remarkable on its own terms. But what made it genuinely revolutionary was what happened next: Paabo's team found Denisovan DNA inside living people.

Between four and six percent of the genome of modern **Melanesians**, the indigenous peoples of **Papua New Guinea**, the **Solomon Islands**, and surrounding territories, turned out to be Denisovan in origin. This was not a trace signal. This was a substantial genetic contribution from a species that no anthropologist had ever described from a skeleton, that had no entry in any textbook, and that was known to science from a single finger bone and two teeth found in a Siberian cave thousands of miles from Melanesia. The implication was unambiguous. Denisovans and modern humans had not merely coexisted; they had interbred, repeatedly and successfully, producing children who survived and whose descendants carry Denisovan DNA to this day. The geographic puzzle, how Denisovan genes could have traveled from Siberia to Papua New Guinea, suggested that the Denisovan range was far larger than the single cave site indicated, or that both populations had moved through territories that are now underwater, submerged by the post-glacial sea-level rise that flooded Sundaland and the land bridges of Southeast Asia.

The same year, Paabo's group confirmed what had been suspected since the first Neanderthal genome was published in draft form in 2010: non-African modern humans carry between one and four percent Neanderthal DNA. A **2024 Nature paper** refined the timing of this admixture with unprecedented precision, pinpointing the primary interbreeding event to approximately 45,000 to 49,000 years ago, somewhere in western Asia, during the narrow window when expanding *Homo sapiens* populations and retreating Neanderthal groups overlapped geographically. The genetic signatures of Neanderthal admixture are not evenly distributed across the human genome; they are concentrated in genes related to immune function and skin pigmentation, suggesting that the Neanderthal alleles provided adaptive advantages that natural selection preserved. A **2025 ScienceDaily report** described additional Denisovan introgression events shaping modern populations in South and Southeast Asia, with at least three separate episodes of interbreeding leaving distinct genetic signatures in different regional populations. The Denisovans, it turned out, were not a single homogeneous group but a diverse lineage, or perhaps multiple lineages, that had inhabited Asia for hundreds of thousands of years.

None of this was suppressed. None of it was hidden. None of it was fought by institutional gatekeepers or buried in obscure journals. The Denisovan discovery was published in the most prestigious journal in science and covered on the front page of newspapers worldwide. Paabo received the **Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine** in 2022, specifically for his work on ancient DNA and the revelation of archaic human interbreeding. The **Smithsonian Human Origins Program** incorporated the findings into its permanent exhibits within months of publication. The discovery of Denisovans, the confirmation of Neanderthal admixture, the identification of what geneticists call **ghost populations** (archaic lineages detectable in DNA but invisible in the fossil record) are among the most celebrated scientific achievements of the twenty-first century.

This matters, and it matters specifically in the context of this book, because one of the recurring claims in alternative history circles is that mainstream science suppresses evidence of "anomalous" human lineages. The ancient DNA revolution, which has transformed every aspect of human origins research since 2010, demonstrates exactly the opposite. When the evidence is real, when it is produced through verifiable methods and published in peer-reviewed journals with open data and replicable protocols, the scientific establishment does not suppress it. It awards it the Nobel Prize.


### The Bone Bed at Paluxy

The evidence that mainstream science actually does reject tends to come from a different category entirely: evidence that does not survive scrutiny.

In 1908, a teenager named **George Adams** noticed unusual markings in the exposed limestone along the **Paluxy River** near **Glen Rose, Texas**. The Cretaceous limestone, roughly 113 million years old, was littered with three-toed dinosaur tracks, which local farmers had known about for years. The tracks were well-preserved and, in some cases, spectacular: deep, clearly defined impressions in rock that had once been soft river mud, showing the weight distribution and gait of animals that had been dead for over a hundred million years. But Adams claimed to see something else among the tracks: human footprints, full-sized, five-toed, walking alongside the dinosaurs.

The claim electrified the young-earth creationist community. If humans and dinosaurs had walked together, the entire geological timescale was wrong, and with it the framework of evolutionary biology. The **Paluxy tracks** became, for several decades, one of the most cited pieces of physical evidence against mainstream geology. **Carl Baugh**, an evangelist and self-styled archaeologist whose academic credentials came from an unaccredited correspondence school, opened the **Creation Evidence Museum** near Glen Rose in 1984, built largely around the Paluxy claims. The museum displayed casts of the tracks and promoted them as definitive proof of human-dinosaur coexistence. For creationists, the Paluxy River was Exhibit A: the physical smoking gun that proved the scientific establishment had been wrong, not merely wrong in detail or emphasis, but catastrophically wrong about the basic timeline of life on Earth.

The scientific investigation of the Paluxy tracks was thorough and, for the creationist case, devastating. **Glen Kuban**, an independent researcher who spent years documenting the site with meticulous photography and casting, demonstrated that the "human" prints were elongated dinosaur tracks. Many theropod dinosaurs walked on three toes but occasionally left impressions of the full metatarsus, the elongated foot structure behind the toes, producing oblong prints that, when partially eroded or filled with secondary sediment, could resemble a human foot to an untrained eye. Some of the tracks showed faint color differences in the limestone that revealed the three-toed dinosaur footprint hidden within the apparently human outline. Kuban also documented that some of the tracks had been deliberately altered: chisel marks were visible on the limestone, and one set of "giant human prints" turned out to have been carved during the **Great Depression** by local residents who sold them to tourists for fifteen dollars apiece. The carver, George Adams himself (then elderly), reportedly acknowledged this to researchers in the 1970s. The tracks had been a cottage industry before they became a theological argument.

The **Institute for Creation Research**, initially one of the most vocal promoters of the Paluxy tracks, eventually withdrew its support. In 1986, the organization published a statement acknowledging that the evidence was insufficient and should not be used in creationist arguments. This was not the behavior of an institution that suppresses inconvenient data; this was an organization on the alternative side of the debate recognizing that its own evidence had failed.

The **Ica stones**, a collection of engraved andesite pebbles from Peru depicting dinosaurs, brain surgery, telescopes, and advanced technology, followed a similar trajectory from sensation to debunking. **Javier Cabrera Darquea**, a physician in the town of **Ica**, amassed a collection of over 15,000 stones beginning in the 1960s, which he displayed in a private museum and cited as proof of an advanced ancient civilization that coexisted with dinosaurs and possessed technologies that would not be reinvented for millennia. The stones depicted species-specific dinosaurs with anatomical detail that appeared to rule out coincidence. In 1973, the farmer who had been supplying many of the stones, **Basilio Uschuya**, admitted to carving them himself, using images from comic books, encyclopedias, and natural history magazines as references. He demonstrated his carving technique on camera, using a dental drill and a blowtorch to age the surface. Cabrera dismissed the confession, arguing that Uschuya had been pressured by authorities who feared the implications of the stones for Peru's tourism narrative, but subsequent analysis showed modern tool marks inconsistent with the claimed antiquity. Some genuine pre-Columbian engraved stones do exist in Peru, but none depict dinosaurs. The Ica collection, in its entirety, fails every test of provenance that archaeology requires.

The Cambodian temple carvings at **Ta Prohm**, claimed by various alternative researchers to depict a **stegosaurus**, complete the set of frequently cited human-dinosaur evidence. The carving in question is a small decorative medallion, roughly thirty centimeters across, on a twelfth-century **Khmer** temple. When examined at full resolution rather than in the carefully cropped photographs that circulate online, the "stegosaurus" is more consistent with the stylized depictions of animals (possibly a rhinoceros or a wild boar amid decorative foliage) found throughout Khmer temple art. The supposed dorsal plates are surrounded by a frame of leaves and berries identical to those framing the other animal medallions on the same wall. **Pareidolia**, the human tendency to recognize familiar patterns in ambiguous stimuli, does the rest.

The fossil record across every continent shows a consistent stratigraphic separation between dinosaur-bearing **Mesozoic** strata and human-bearing **Quaternary** deposits. The gap is approximately 65.7 million years. No credible evidence has ever closed it.


### The Mammoth Hunters

If the human-dinosaur coexistence claim is a clear case of fabricated and debunked evidence, the question of how humans interacted with Pleistocene megafauna occupies genuinely contested scientific ground. And the debate that plays out within mainstream paleontology is, in many respects, more interesting than the fringe claims it is often confused with.

Paul Martin, an ecologist at the University of Arizona, proposed the **overkill hypothesis** in 1966. The argument was elegant and disturbing in its simplicity. When modern humans arrived on a new continent, the large animals that had never encountered human hunters went extinct within a few thousand years. The pattern repeated everywhere. Australia lost its giant marsupials, including **Diprotodon** (a wombat the size of a rhinoceros), **Thylacoleo** (a marsupial lion), and **Genyornis** (a bird that stood over two meters tall and weighed roughly a quarter of a ton), after humans arrived roughly 50,000 years ago. North America lost its mammoths, mastodons, ground sloths, saber-toothed cats, American camels, American horses, giant beavers, and dozens of other species within about two millennia of the Clovis hunters' appearance around 13,000 years ago. The Caribbean islands lost their giant sloths almost precisely when the first canoe-borne settlers reached them. Madagascar lost its giant lemurs and elephant birds within centuries of Polynesian arrival. New Zealand lost its moa within decades of Maori settlement. Wherever humans showed up for the first time, the large animals vanished.

Martin called this pattern **Pleistocene overkill**. The mechanism was what ecologists call "naive prey": animals that had evolved without exposure to human predation had no fear response, no evasive behavior, no evolutionary preparation for a bipedal hunter with projectile weapons and the cognitive capacity for coordinated group hunting. Modern island species provide depressing confirmation. The great auk, the dodo, the Steller's sea cow, the moa of New Zealand were all exterminated by human hunters within decades or centuries of first contact. They simply did not know to run.

**Sergei Lisitsyn** (Сергей Лисицын), a candidate of historical sciences and senior research fellow in the Department of Paleolithic at the **Institute for the History of Material Culture** of the Russian Academy of Sciences, offered a more textured view of the human-megafauna relationship in a presentation for Antropogenez (Антропогенез). Lisitsyn opened by pointing out a fact that museum displays tend to obscure: the averaged mammoth was actually smaller than modern African elephants. This surprises most people, who picture mammoths as the towering beasts of museum dioramas and animated films. The imperial mammoth of North America and the **Sungari mammoth** of East Asia were genuinely enormous, weighing up to twelve tons, but **Cretan dwarf mammoths** and their counterparts on the **Channel Islands** of California were the size of a cow, a product of island dwarfism operating over thousands of generations. The species' range and morphological diversity was extraordinary, and any generalization about "the mammoth" conceals as much as it reveals.

Lisitsyn complicated the simple overkill picture by detailing the archaeological evidence for how Paleolithic humans actually interacted with mammoths. At excavation sites in Russia, his team found evidence that humans used mammoth ivory as a raw material with remarkable sophistication: tusks were used as what archaeologists call a "core" or nucleus, from which narrow blanks were carefully split using techniques analogous to stone-tool napping, then shaped into tools, ornaments, and other objects. This is an extraordinarily rare type of find, and it reveals something the overkill narrative misses: humans in timber-scarce ice-age environments treated mammoths not only as prey but as a source of industrial raw material. The technology required to split precise, uniform strips from a mammoth tusk, which is denser and more elastic than most stone, is not trivial. It implies accumulated knowledge, specialized technique, and a degree of material sophistication that complicates the image of humans as simple predators.

At another site, **Khotylevo 1**, Lisitsyn's team found Middle Paleolithic bone tools, evidence that Neanderthals had hunted and butchered a wild horse. The cut marks on the skull told a precise story: the Neanderthals had extracted the tongue (a classic delicacy in hunter-gatherer foodways, rich in fat and easy to remove from a large carcass) and possibly the fat pad beneath the mane, a resource prized by pastoral nomads to this day and still incorporated into the cuisines of several Central Asian peoples. The evidence suggests that both Neanderthals and modern humans were competent, knowledgeable predators of large animals, but their strategies were opportunistic, varied, and informed by deep familiarity with the anatomy and behavior of their prey. The word "overkill" implies a blunt, unsophisticated process. The archaeology suggests something far more nuanced.


### The Climate Counterargument

A **2021 Nature Communications study** made the most rigorous case to date that climate change, not human hunting, was the primary driver of North American megafaunal extinction. The study correlated high-resolution paleoclimate data with the extinction chronology of individual species and found that climate fluctuations, specifically the rapid warming and habitat disruption at the end of the Pleistocene, tracked more closely with extinction timing than human arrival dates for most species. The mammoth steppe, the vast grassland ecosystem that supported the megafauna of the Northern Hemisphere, did not simply shrink during the post-glacial warming; it fragmented, leaving isolated populations in habitats that were too small to sustain viable breeding groups. Climate change, in this model, was the executioner; human hunting was at most a contributing factor that pushed already-declining populations over the edge.

The argument is not that humans played no role. Virtually no serious paleontologist argues that human hunting pressure was zero. The argument is about relative weight: was it primarily humans, primarily climate, or some combination weighted differently for different species and different continents? The evidence, honestly assessed, supports different answers for different cases. Australia, where the climate was relatively stable during the period of human arrival and megafaunal extinction, looks like a strong case for overkill. North America, where massive climatic shifts coincided with human arrival, looks like a case where both factors were at work. Eurasia, where humans and megafauna coexisted for tens of thousands of years before the extinctions, looks like a case where climate may have been the dominant factor. The field continues to debate the weighting, and this ongoing debate is conducted entirely within mainstream science. It is not a fringe claim. It is not suppressed. It is the normal operation of science confronting genuinely complex evidence.

Richards, who investigated both sides of related debates with his usual refusal to grant either camp a clean victory, pointed to the complexity of the evidence in a different domain that illuminates the methodological challenges. In his analysis of claims made during the **Hancock ([Chapter 23](23-the-clovis-wall.md))-Dibble ([see Chapter 35](35-the-saa-letter.md)) debate** on the Joe Rogan Experience, Richards noted that the genetic record of plant domestication is far messier than most debaters acknowledge. Rice, in particular, shows multiple genetic pathways to the same domestication traits, including redundant **quantitative trait loci** controlling **seed shattering** (the mechanism by which wild grains drop their seeds before harvest). A 2021 paper on **feralization**, published under the title "When a Crop Grows Back to the Wild," demonstrated that domesticated crops can revert to wild phenotypes within centuries, not the "thousands of years" stated with confidence on a major podcast. The genetic record of rice shows signatures consistent with at least one cycle of domestication followed by feralization and re-domestication, a finding whose implications are, to put it mildly, inconvenient for anyone claiming certainty about the agricultural history of the deep past.<sup>[1](../ENDNOTES.md#sources-chapter-34)</sup><sup>[2](../ENDNOTES.md#sources-chapter-34)</sup>

The relevance to the megafauna debate is methodological rather than substantive. If the genetic evidence for events that occurred 10,000 to 15,000 years ago is ambiguous enough that leading researchers cannot agree on whether rice was domesticated once or multiple times, then confident assertions about the cause of megafaunal extinction in the same time period deserve a degree of humility that is not always evident in the debate.<sup>[3](../ENDNOTES.md#sources-chapter-34)</sup>

Richards made a broader observation about the dynamics of the plant domestication question that applies equally to the overkill controversy. Dibble stated on air that it would take "thousands of years" for domesticated plants to revert to wild forms. Richards demonstrated, citing published feralization research, that this claim was inaccurate: visible reversion can occur within a hundred years, and by a thousand years the genetic signature may be undetectable. Dibble, when challenged on social media, omitted the "thousands of years" claim from his defense entirely, stating only that he had told Rogan he did not know the answer. Richards posted the clip. Both statements were on the record. The point is not that Dibble is a dishonest actor. The point is that confident, authoritative claims about deep-time biological processes are often less settled than they appear in a debate format, and the same caution applies to the overkill hypothesis.<sup>[4](../ENDNOTES.md#sources-chapter-34)</sup><sup>[5](../ENDNOTES.md#sources-chapter-34)</sup>


### The Ghost Populations

The ancient DNA revolution has revealed something else that complicates the clean narrative of human origins: ghost populations. These are human lineages that left genetic traces in modern populations but have no corresponding fossil record. They are invisible to archaeology and visible only to genetics.<sup>[6](../ENDNOTES.md#sources-chapter-34)</sup>

In 2012, a team led by **David Reich** at **Harvard University** identified a ghost population they called **Ancient North Eurasians**, an ancestral group that contributed significant amounts of DNA to both modern Europeans and Native Americans but left no known fossil remains anywhere on Earth. The population was identified purely through statistical analysis of genetic variation; no skull, no tooth, no fragment of bone has ever been attributed to it. In 2014, another ghost population was identified in the DNA of West African populations, a lineage that diverged from the modern human line between 200,000 and 300,000 years ago and interbred with the ancestors of modern West Africans as recently as 20,000 years ago. This population has no name, no fossils, and no archaeological signature. It exists only as a statistical signal in the genomes of living people.

The implication is striking. The fossil record, which for most of the twentieth century was assumed to provide a reasonably complete picture of human evolution, is riddled with gaps. Species that were genetically distinct, that lived alongside modern humans for tens of thousands of years, that interbred with our ancestors producing children who passed their genes to us, left no bones that have ever been found, or at least no bones that have been recognized as different. The discovery of Homo floresiensis in 2003, a small-bodied hominin that lived on the island of Flores in Indonesia until approximately 50,000 years ago, confirmed what the genetic evidence had already suggested: genuinely different human species coexisted with *Homo sapiens* far more recently than the textbooks of the 1990s allowed. Floresiensis stood roughly one meter tall, had a brain about the size of a chimpanzee's, and was found alongside stone tools that suggested competent, if not sophisticated, technology. As researchers in the Cosmic Summit community noted, this was among the most extraordinary mainstream discoveries of the twenty-first century: a different kind of human, living on an island, completely unknown to science until a team from the **Australian National University** opened the right cave on the right island.

Hancock used these discoveries as supporting evidence for his lost civilization hypothesis in *America Before*, arguing that the existence of unknown archaic lineages and population bottlenecks corresponding to catastrophic events suggested a deeper, more complex human past than mainstream science acknowledged. The argument has a surface plausibility that dissolves on closer inspection. The archaic lineages discovered through ancient DNA (Denisovans, Neanderthals, the unnamed West African ghost population) are known archaic hominins, not evidence of an advanced civilization. Their genetics are fully integrated into mainstream models of human evolution. The population bottlenecks Hancock references are associated with well-understood climatic events (ice ages, volcanic eruptions, the Younger Dryas cooling), not with the collapse of a civilization for which no material evidence exists.<sup>[7](../ENDNOTES.md#sources-chapter-34)</sup>

The distinction matters. Hancock's rhetorical move is to treat each genuinely surprising scientific discovery as evidence that "science was wrong" and therefore that his specific alternative (a lost Ice Age civilization) becomes more plausible. But science being wrong about the timing of Clovis settlement does not make science wrong about whether an advanced civilization existed during the Ice Age. Science being surprised by Denisovans does not mean the next surprise will be Atlantis. Each claim must be evaluated on its own evidence, and the evidence for an Ice Age civilization remains what it has always been: absent.


### The Uncut Pages

Sklyarov ([Chapter 1](01-the-tolerance-problem.md)) approached the question of human origins from a framework so different from mainstream genetics that the two barely occupy the same conversation. In ***Генетический код человечества*** (The Genetic Code of Humanity), Sklyarov presented an extended critique of Darwinian evolution, drawing heavily on the work of Russian evolutionary theorist **Yuri Chaikovsky** (Юрий Чайковский).<sup>[8](../ENDNOTES.md#sources-chapter-34)</sup>

Sklyarov's argument, or more precisely Chaikovsky's argument as Sklyarov deployed it, attacked several foundational premises of Darwinian theory with a vigor that made Western creationist critiques look like polite disagreements. Chaikovsky had pointed out that Malthus's geometric progression of population growth, which Darwin adopted as the theoretical engine of natural selection through competition for resources, was contradicted by empirical evidence. The detail that Chaikovsky presented was, characteristically, more specific than the argument required. He noted that Darwin himself had barely read Malthus: «Увы, саму книгу Мальтуса почти никто в руки не брал, и даже в личной библиотеке Дарвина первый ее том не был расчеркан, второй же остался неразрезанным» (Alas, almost nobody actually picked up Malthus's book, and even in Darwin's personal library the first volume was unmarked, while the second remained uncut). In the nineteenth century, uncut pages were the equivalent of an unbroken seal on a book's spine: they meant the reader had never gotten that far. Darwin had built the theoretical foundation of natural selection on a book whose second half he apparently never read.<sup>[9](../ENDNOTES.md#sources-chapter-34)</sup>

Chaikovsky went further. He cited research showing that animal populations do not, in fact, grow geometrically until constrained by resources; they self-regulate through mechanisms that remain poorly understood. Sklyarov amplified this with a striking series of experiments with rats confined in isolated environments with unlimited food, water, and space to breed. The rats initially reproduced rapidly, reaching a population of about a thousand individuals, then plateaued briefly, then fell into apathy. The animals stopped breeding, stopped engaging in social behavior, and the entire population went extinct, even though the environment could have supported tens of thousands. Even the most active individuals, when isolated separately and given fresh conditions, still succumbed to the same trajectory. The experiment was repeated multiple times with consistent results. Sklyarov presented this as evidence against the Malthusian premises underlying Darwinian selection: if populations self-regulate and self-destruct even in conditions of abundance, the engine of natural selection (competition for scarce resources) has no fuel to burn.<sup>[10](../ENDNOTES.md#sources-chapter-34)</sup><sup>[11](../ENDNOTES.md#sources-chapter-34)</sup>

The critique is not without merit as intellectual history. Chaikovsky was a respected historian of science in Russia, and his observations about the gap between Malthus's theoretical model and empirical population dynamics are shared by some Western evolutionary biologists, particularly those working in the tradition of **Stephen Jay Gould's** punctuated equilibrium. But the leap from "certain premises of Darwin's original formulation require updating" to "therefore evolution does not explain human origins, and an alternative creative intervention is required" is a leap that neither Chaikovsky's data nor Sklyarov's argument supports. Modern evolutionary theory has incorporated population genetics, epigenetics, horizontal gene transfer, neutral theory, kin selection, and dozens of other mechanisms that Darwin could not have anticipated. The theory has been updated continuously for 160 years. That updates were necessary does not invalidate the framework, any more than the discovery of general relativity invalidated Newtonian mechanics for the domains where Newton's equations work perfectly well.<sup>[12](../ENDNOTES.md#sources-chapter-34)</sup>

Sklyarov was a more careful thinker than this summary might suggest. He accurately cited **Fleeming Jenkin's** 1867 critique of Darwin, which Chaikovsky called "the first successful attempt at a logical analysis of Darwinism." Jenkin had pointed out that a single favorable mutation in one individual would be "swallowed by the swamp" of ordinary organisms through interbreeding within a few generations, unable to propagate unless it appeared simultaneously in a large percentage of the population. This was a genuinely devastating objection that Darwin never satisfactorily answered; it was not resolved until the rediscovery of **Mendelian genetics** in 1900, which showed that hereditary traits do not blend but are transmitted as discrete units. Sklyarov noted Darwin's own admission about the misleading nature of the term "chance": «Я до сих пор иногда говорил так, как будто изменения... были делом случая. Это, конечно, совершенно неверное выражение, но оно служит для показа нашего незнания причины каждого конкретного изменения» (I have hitherto sometimes spoken as if the variations... had been due to chance. This, of course, is a completely incorrect expression, but it serves to acknowledge our ignorance of the cause of each particular variation). Darwin himself understood that "random" was a placeholder for "not yet understood," not a description of the phenomenon's actual nature.<sup>[13](../ENDNOTES.md#sources-chapter-34)</sup>

The problem with Sklyarov's deployment of these critiques is not that the critiques are wrong; some of them are genuinely incisive observations about the limits of mid-nineteenth-century evolutionary theory. The problem is the conclusion drawn from them: that because natural selection has explanatory gaps, an external creative intelligence must be invoked. This is the same logical structure as the "god of the gaps" argument, and it carries the same vulnerability. Every time science fills a gap, the argument retreats to the next one. Jenkin's devastating objection was resolved by Mendel. The resolution of the next gap will leave Sklyarov's framework with one less place to stand.<sup>[14](../ENDNOTES.md#sources-chapter-34)</sup>


### The Weight of Bone

The honest summary of the evidence covered in this chapter is simpler than any party to the debate would prefer.

Ancient DNA research has transformed the understanding of human origins. The discoveries of Denisovans, Neanderthal admixture, ghost populations, and archaic introgression are among the most significant scientific achievements of the twenty-first century. They are published openly, awarded the highest honors in science, and integrated into mainstream models of human evolution. The claim that these findings are suppressed or hidden is flatly contradicted by the evidence. The Nobel committee does not give prizes for work it is trying to bury.

Human-dinosaur coexistence is not supported by any credible evidence. The Paluxy tracks, the Ica stones, and the Ta Prohm carvings have been specifically investigated and refuted. The fossil record maintains a consistent 65.7-million-year separation between non-avian dinosaurs and anatomically modern humans across every continent. The organizations that originally promoted the Paluxy evidence subsequently retracted their support. No new evidence has emerged to replace what was debunked.

**The overkill debate** around megafaunal extinction is a genuine, ongoing scientific controversy conducted entirely within mainstream paleontology. Paul Martin's overkill hypothesis is well-supported for some continents and time periods but contested for others. Climate change, human hunting, and their interaction all play roles whose relative weight varies by species and geography. No credible peer-reviewed research attributes the extinctions to a catastrophic civilizational event or to a suppressed external cause. The debate is active, productive, and unresolved, which is what an active scientific debate looks like from the inside.

Sklyarov's critique of Darwinian evolution draws on legitimate intellectual history and presents some genuinely incisive observations, particularly regarding the gap between Malthusian theory and empirical population dynamics. But the critique deploys these observations in service of a conclusion (external creative intervention) that the evidence does not support and that the historical trajectory of biology consistently undermines. The gaps in evolutionary theory are real. They are also being filled, steadily, by the normal operation of science.

The bone fragment from Denisova Cave weighed less than a gram. The species it revealed had been invisible for a million years. It was found by chance, identified by technology that did not exist a decade earlier, and published in the most visible scientific journal on Earth. The Nobel committee awarded the prize. The textbooks were rewritten. The story went the way it went.
